
# PROJECT: GenomIQ Research Hub
## Personal PhD Research Intelligence and Management Platform

### YOUR ROLE

Act as a senior full-stack software engineer, bioinformatics
software architect, research informatics specialist and UX designer.

Your task is to design and build a fully functional, free,
personal research management and literature intelligence
platform called GenomIQ Research Hub.

Do not build a static mockup or a dashboard containing
dummy information.

Build a working application with a functioning database,
real academic literature integrations, automated discovery,
literature recommendations, research documentation,
progress tracking and export functionality.

You have access to the development environment, terminal,
browser and relevant tools. Use them to inspect, develop,
test and verify the application.

Prioritise free and open-source technologies.

Do not introduce paid APIs, subscriptions or services as
mandatory dependencies.

4. INTELLIGENT PAPER RECOMMENDATION ENGINE
==================================================

This is a core feature.

The supervisor expects the user to read FOUR papers
every week.

Develop an intelligent recommendation engine that
selects four papers every week.

Do not simply recommend the newest four publications.

Use a weighted recommendation algorithm.

Consider:

1. Relevance to the user's PhD research.
2. Relevance to active research topics.
3. Publication recency.
4. Scientific influence and citation information.
5. Methodological relevance.
6. Foundational importance.
7. Novelty of findings.
8. Relationship to previously read papers.
9. Whether the user has already read the paper.

Initial weekly allocation:

- Two papers: Core research topics.
- One paper: Computational or methodological topics.
- One paper: Complementary research topics.

Allow the allocation to be customised.

The recommendation engine should distinguish:

- Foundational papers.
- Landmark papers.
- Recent research.
- Review articles.
- Methodological papers.
- Experimental studies.
- Critical or contrasting studies.

RECOMMENDATION EXPLANATION:

Every recommended paper must include:

1. Why this paper was selected.
2. Which research interest it addresses.
3. Its relevance to the PhD.
4. Three important things to learn from it.
5. Whether it is foundational, recent or methodological.
6. Suggested prerequisite papers where applicable.

Do not fabricate recommendation explanations.

Base recommendations on available metadata, abstracts
and accessible article content.

Provide an option to generate a reading pathway
from introductory papers to advanced publications.

The user must be able to accept, reject, postpone
or replace recommendations.

Do not recommend the same paper repeatedly unless
it is intentionally marked for revision.
